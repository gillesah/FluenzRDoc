# Open tracking and consent

Open tracking relies on a pixel: an invisible one by one pixel image added at the
bottom of the email. When the recipient's mail client loads it, FluenzR records that
the message was opened.

Since 2026, this mechanism is regulated in France. This page explains what changed and
what FluenzR gives you to deal with it.

> This page describes a legal framework and the tools FluenzR provides. It is not legal
> advice: as the sender, you are the data controller for your own campaigns.

## What changed

In deliberation no. 2026-042 of 12 March 2026, published in the Official Journal on
14 April 2026, the CNIL, the French data protection authority, adopted a recommendation
treating email tracking pixels as trackers falling under **article 82 of the French Data
Protection Act**, the same article that governs cookies.

The consequence: measuring opens **to optimise your campaigns** requires the recipient's
consent. A transition period ran until 14 July 2026.

## Exemptions

Not every use requires consent. The recommendation notably provides for:

| Purpose | Consent |
|---------|---------|
| Measuring opens to optimise campaigns | Required |
| Deliverability: detecting inactive contacts to adjust frequency or clean the list | Exempt, with conditions |
| Security: authentication, password reset | Exempt |
| Transactional emails tied to a requested service | Exempt |

The deliverability exemption is the most useful in practice, but it comes with a
condition: only **the date of last open** may be retained.

> **That is exactly what FluenzR does.** The pixel records nothing but an open date, on
> the first open. No IP address, no user agent, no reopen counter, no geolocation. If
> your use stays within removing inactive contacts, you fall inside the exemption.

## What FluenzR gives you

### Turning tracking off for your whole account

In **Account settings**, uncheck **Open tracking**. No pixel will be injected into your
emails any more, in campaigns or direct sends.

This setting only affects the pixel. Still in place:

- click tracking, which does not fall under the same regime;
- the unsubscribe link, required under the GDPR;
- your postal address, required under CAN-SPAM.

When tracking is off, the open rate is no longer shown on the dashboard or in campaign
statistics. A label says so, to avoid reading a zero as poor performance.

### Informing recipients

When the pixel is active, FluenzR automatically adds a notice to the footer of every
email stating that it is present, what it is for, and offering a link to object to it.
Nothing to configure.

### Recipient objections

The link in the notice leads to the preferences page, the same one used for
unsubscribing. The recipient finds two distinct actions there:

- **unsubscribe**: they receive nothing further;
- **object to open tracking**: they keep receiving your emails, without the pixel.

The objection is stored on the contact record and applies to all your campaigns
immediately. It always takes precedence over your account setting: even with tracking
on, a contact who objected receives no pixel.

## What remains yours to handle

FluenzR gives you the tools, not compliance itself. Still on you:

- **your privacy policy**, which must describe the pixel, its purpose and the retention
  period;
- **the legal basis for your sends**, separate from the pixel: consent to receive your
  emails is documented on the contact record, in the **Consent source** field;
- **informing your existing lists**, if you email contacts collected before April 2026.

## Frequently asked

**Do I need to email people asking for consent to the pixel?**

No. The recommendation describes information plus a right to object, not a prior
opt-in. A recipient who says nothing stays tracked. That is what the footer notice does.

**Is click tracking affected?**

The recommendation targets the tracking pixel. Click tracking follows a deliberate
action by the recipient, and FluenzR leaves it on in every case.

**Does a contact who objected still receive my emails?**

Yes. That is the difference with unsubscribing: they lose the pixel, not the messages.
